
# Stendahl Art Galleries Records: Data Visualisations

**Exploring the archival records of the Stendahl Art Galleries through data visualisation and digital research.**

This repository contains interactive data visualisations developed during my role as Research Specialist for the Pre-Hispanic Art Provenance Initiative (PHAPI), focusing on the **Stendahl Art Galleries Records (2017.M.38)**, held at the Getty Research Institute, Los Angeles.

The Stendahl Art Galleries records document the activities of one of the most influential dealers in the twentieth-century market for pre-Hispanic art.

Since 2019, the Getty Research Institute's **Pre-Hispanic Art Provenance Initiative (PHAPI)** has investigated the international art market's commodification of pre-Hispanic art and antiquities during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. The initiative examines the looting, commodification, collecting, and circulation of pre-Hispanic material culture, and has undertaken the digitisation, transcription, and analysis of key archival materials, including the Stendahl Inventory Books and Family Correspondence.

The project is particularly interested in:

- The geographical networks associated with the Stendahl Art Galleries.
- The relationships between dealers, collectors, institutions, and other market participants.
- The movement and circulation of pre-Hispanic objects through international art markets.
- The interpretative potential of archival silences, missing information, and documentary absences.
- The use of digital methods to support provenance research and the study of twentieth-century collecting practices.

This repository builds on the archival resources made available through the initiative, using data visualisation and exploratory digital methods to investigate the geographical, documentary, and commercial networks represented in the Stendahl Art Galleries records.

## Visualisations

### SAG_Inventory Books_Locations_Map

An exploratory visualisation examining the geographical dimension of the Stendahl Art Galleries records, considering documented locations alongside lacunae and ambiguity in the surviving archival evidence.

**[View the visualisation](https://kagking.github.io/Stendahl-Art-Galleries-Records_Data-Visualisations/SAG_Inventory%20Books_Locations_Map.html)**

### SAG_Family Correspondence_Place of Origin_Map

An interactive visualisation exploring the geographical place of origin for Family Correspondence preserved in the archive (concentrated between the mid-1950s and mid-1960s).

The visualisation uses the correspondence as a point of departure for examining the geographical reach of Earl Stendahl's personal and professional networks.

**[View the visualisation](https://kagking.github.io/Stendahl-Art-Galleries-Records_Data-Visualisations/SAG_Family%20Correspondence_Place%20of%20Origin_Map.html)**

### SAG_Inventory Books_Object Types

An interactive visualisation of object types recorded in the Stendahl Art Galleries Inventory Books, illustrating the range and distribution of objects documented across the gallery's inventory records.

**[View the visualisation](https://raw.githack.com/kagking/Stendahl-Art-Galleries-Records_Data-Visualisations/main/SAG_Inventory%20Books_Object%20Types.html)**

## Research Approach

This work uses digital methods to explore archival material beyond conventional document-based research. Data visualisation provides a means of identifying patterns, relationships, and geographical connections while also drawing attention to the limitations of the surviving evidence.

## Content Advisory

Some archival records reproduce historical terminology and descriptions that are biased, inaccurate, or derogatory, particularly in relation to Indigenous peoples and cultures. Such language reflects the historical context of the source material and should not be understood as endorsed terminology.

Please consult the [Getty Research Institute's transcription project](https://fromthepage.com/gri/stendahl-art-galleries-archival-transcription-project) for further information about the archival material, transcription methodology, and content advisory.
